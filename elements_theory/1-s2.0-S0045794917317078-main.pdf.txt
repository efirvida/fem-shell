1. Introduction

This paper presents a review of the formulation of four plate bending quadrilateral elements and new
advanced validation test results. The four elements are DKQ, MITC4, DSQ and DKMQ published between
1982 and 1993. The ﬁrst is valid for thin plates and the three others include transverse shear effects. All
elements have 4 nodes and 3 dof per node (one displacement and two rotations) and give good results in
classical benchmark and patch tests for thin plates. The paper presents the respective formulation of the
four elements in a uniﬁed and comparative manner, starting from the formulation of the classical DKQ
element published in 1982. The paper includes new convergence behavior analysis using the s-norm
tests. The numerical results using DKMQ, DSQ and MITC4 elements show the optimal and uniform con-
vergence for thin and thick plates with a slight advantage for DKMQ due to quadratic terms for the rota-
tion variables.

The phenomenon of shear locking in plate bending ﬁnite ele-
ments based on Reissner-Mindlin theory [1,2] has been the subject
of a large number of theses and papers during the last 45 years. To
overcome this numerical phenomenon, reduced and selective inte-
gration [3–5] in displacement formulations have been used with
incomplete success considering slenderness ratio, element distor-
tion,
individual element test, etc. Mixed formulation [6] and
Assumed Natural Strain (ANS) proposed by Hughes and Tezduyar
[7] and MacNeal [8] have been better alternatives to overcome
the problem of shear locking. ANS has been found a very effective
method used by many authors to develop new ﬁnite elements
based on Reissner-Mindlin plate theory. One of the simplest and
effective ANS formulation to obtain a 4 nodes, three degrees of
freedom (dof) per node, plate bending element with Transverse
Shear (TS) effects included is due to Bathe and Dvorkin [9,10]
who proposed the effective nowadays well-known MITC4 [11] ele-
ment (Mixed Interpolation of Tensorial Components).

Another approach developed by Batoz and former students was
to include transverse shear effects in the element DKQ (Discrete
Kirchhoff Quadrilateral). DKQ, published in 1982 [12], overcomes
the C1 continuity requirement when considering the Kirchhoff thin
plate theory by using the Reissner-Mindlin theory but neglecting

TS energy and imposing discrete Kirchhoff constraints on edges
(see also [13] for the triangular element DKT). Rotation compo-
nents are based on incomplete quadratic polynomials. Extended
validation based on individual element tests, patch tests, conver-
gence tests have been performed and the DKQ element is included
in the library of several softwares. Hence based on the DKQ ele-
ment Batoz and Lardeur proposed the quadrilateral element called
DSQ [14,15] (Discrete Shear Quadrilateral) still with 3 dof per node,
where TS effects have been considered using element equilibrium
equations and shear constitutive equations to deﬁne constant
shear strains along the four edges of the element (see also
[16,17] for the triangular version DST). DST and DSQ elements give
overall good behavior for the analysis of isotropic and composites
thin to thick plates but the transverse shear contribution is a bit
complicated and patch tests for very thick plates were not fully
satisﬁed.

Sharing several aspects of the formulation of DKQ and DSQ,
Katili
[18] proposed in 1993 the element DKMQ (Discrete
Kirchoff-Mindlin Quadrilateral) using simpliﬁed assumed trans-
verse shear strains along the element sides (see also the formula-
tion of the companion triangular element DKMT [19]. Both DKMT
and DKMQ are valid for thin to thick plates, with good convergence
properties and full satisfaction of the patch tests for constant
curvature strain ﬁelds. Elements DST, DKMT, DSQ and DKMQ are
free of shear locking by element constructions as they reduce
respectively to DKT and DKQ for thin plates. Their difference relies
in the representation of the transverse shear strain energy. The

application of the DKMQ element in composite structures, stochas-
tic and buckling analysis is presented in [20–22]. The extension of
DKMQ plate element to a DKMQ24 shell element using Naghdi-
Reissner-Mindlin shell theory taking into account warping effects
and bending-membrane coupling is presented in [23–27].

A new development based on modiﬁed Timoshenko beam the-
ory and modiﬁed Reissner-Mindlin plate theory has been proposed
recently for beam and plate bending problems [28–32]. With this
approach the shear locking problem does not occur due to the
strong interdependence between the bending displacement and
rotations.

Up to the middle of the 1990s the engineering community
involved in ﬁnite element development technology was mainly
emphasizing on individual element tests (proper rank for exam-
ple), patch tests for constant stress situations, convergence analysis
for particular displacements, (or less common stresses), at particu-
lar points of the structure with reference to a known analytical
solution (or more often a numerical solution obtained by a ﬁne
mesh using reference-proved elements), for regular and distorted
meshes. The shear locking phenomenon in plate ﬁnite elements
based on various formulations and in particular mixed and
assumed natural strains did attract the mathematical oriented
community. As a result, new methods and tests to assess element
behavior and convergence have been proposed during the last 25
years. Among others the so called inf-sup and s-norm tests [33–43].
The inf-sup condition for plate bending elements is related to
the veriﬁcation of the lack of shear locking. For the MITC4 element
inf-sup tests have been performed to assess the monotonic and
optimal convergence for thin plates [33–43]. For the DSQ and
DKMQ elements shear locking cannot occur since those elements
converge to DKQ for thin plates. However, a simple indicator for
lack of shear locking has been used in this paper by computing
the eigenvalues of the TS shear stiffness matrix of an individual
element in an inﬁnite mesh to conﬁrm the lack of shear locking.
The technique has been presented in [15,44,45].

The method called s-norm proposed in [33,34,38,39] is an
appropriate method to evaluate the impact of the approximation
ﬁelds and the element formulation on the whole structure. Numer-
ical results based on the s-norm tests can evaluate the capacity for
uniform and optimal convergence. Uniform convergence means
that the convergence does not depend on the thickness of the prob-
lem under study while optimal convergence means that the rate of
convergence and interpolations used in the formulation of the ele-
ment are of the same order. The application of s-norm was exten-
sively used in [40–43] to evaluate various MITC plate and shell
elements.

The paper is organized as follows. Some aspects of the Reissner-
Mindlin plate theory (kinematic, constitutive, equilibrium equa-
tions and modiﬁed Hu-Washizu functional) are recalled in Section 2.
In Sections 3–6 the respective formulation of the elements DKQ,
MITC, DSQ and DKMQ is presented using a uniﬁed notation. Order
of presentation is simply the year of publication. Sections 7 and 8
deal with the pass evaluation tests and s-norm method. In particu-
lar, the convergence behavior of the four elements for various iso-
tropic plate situations using the s-norm is presented in Section 9.
Concluding remarks, acknowledgements and references are given
at the end.

7. Past evaluation tests

The proper rank of the stiffness matrix of a single element (DKQ,
MITC4, DSQ, DKMQ) for any aspect ratio and distortion has been
veriﬁed in previous publications on the four elements. They satisfy
an important ellipticity condition: only three zero eigenvalues rep-
resenting the rigid body modes with the other nine all positive.

Also constant curvatures patch test are satisﬁed for large slen-
derness ratio (say L/h (cid:9) 100) in accordance with the necessary ele-
ment consistency condition.

All four elements are free of shear locking: DKQ by direct ele-
ment formulation, DSQ and DKMQ because they converge to DKQ
for thin plates. MITC4 is also free of shear locking by choosing con-
sistent approximations to represent the shear strains in natural
coordinates. Numerical tests can be used to show the lack of shear
locking: convergence analysis for thin plates for different shapes,
different boundary conditions, different loadings. Results can be
found in the respective references of the elements.

A simple indicator to evaluate the possibility of shear locking is
to compute the rank of the shear stiffness matrix of a single ele-
ment in an assumed inﬁnite mesh (Fig. 8).

The three elements DKMQ, DSQ and MITC4 have been pro-

the transverse displacement w (n, g) is used for all elements to
deﬁne the equivalent load vectors. Regarding MITC4, our results
are in agreement with those published in [33], obtained for thin

We analyze a clamped square plate under uniform loading fz
where the details can be seen on Fig. 9. Because of symmetry, only
area ABCD is considered with hard support conditions w = by = bx =

=0 along DC are taken into account.

10. Concluding remarks

The paper includes detailed formulation aspects of the stiff-
ness matrix of four quadrilateral plate bending elements with
3 dof per node, based on the ﬁrst order Reissner-Mindlin the-
ory. The three elements DKQ, DSQ and DKMQ have been devel-
oped between 1982 and 1993 by the senior author and former
students. The fourth element MITC4 has been published in
1984, as a ﬁrst element of a series of MITC plate and shell ele-
ments developed by Professor Bathe and collaborators. Even
though the formulation of the four elements can be found in
various documents, the present paper is written in order to
understand the differences and complementarities, using a uni-
ﬁed notation and similar assumed shear strain constraints on
element sides. The paper shows that for thin plates DSQ and
DKMQ should converge to DKQ without shear locking. On the
other end for extremely thick plates the formulation of DKMQ
is such that DKMQ (cid:8) MITC4. This last result has never been
shown before.

S-norm tests have been implemented and new results have
been obtained and reported in this paper. The results show that
uniform linear convergence is observed for the four elements
even if the DKQ, DSQ and DKMQ are based on incomplete
quadratic interpolation of the rotations. However, the relative
error in strain energy is slightly smaller in DKQ, DSQ and
DKMQ compared to MITC4 for square plates, either with uni-
form or distorted meshes. Due to the simplicity of formulation,
the DKMQ element is much preferable than DKQ and DSQ since
it is valid for thin to thick plates (not the case for DKQ) and it
fully satisﬁes the requirement for a robust and convergent ele-
ment (satisfying all types of patch tests which is not the case
for DSQ).

The shear energy representation in DKMQ is also simpler than
for DSQ. It is also seen that the contribution of the quadratic terms
of DKMQ can be introduced without much effort to enhance MITC4
for the bending behavior of thin plates.
