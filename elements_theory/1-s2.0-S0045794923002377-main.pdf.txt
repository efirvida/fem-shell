In this work, a novel degenerated shell element called MITC4+R is developed, which is based on the MITC4+ (an 
improved MITC quadrilateral shell element) and reduced integration with one point quadrature. The assumed 
natural  strain  method  is  employed  to  construct  the  membrane  and  shear  strains.  A  physical  stabilization 
term is derivated after the assumed natural strains are decomposed into the linear and a constant term with 
respect to the natural coordinates. The hourglass modes owing to one point quadrature is controlled in the 
computation of deformation by introducing this physical stabilization term. The developed degenerated shell 
element is successfully implemented into the implicit static and explicit dynamic analyses. In order to verify 
its computational accuracy and eﬃciency, several standard benchmarks and practical problems are performed. 
These results show that the MITC4+R element eliminates various locking problems common to shell elements, 
and it signiﬁcantly improves the computational eﬃciency of the original MITC4+ element. Meanwhile, the 
MITC4+R owns the same accuracy and highly consistent convergence as the original MITC4+ element, making 
it more promising for practical applications.

1.  Introduction

Nowadays, the simulation of shell elements has been a critical factor 
in the structural analysis of bodywork or airframe. In general, a mesh 
model  of  the  body-in-white  is  established  mainly  from  quadrilateral 
shell elements. The emergence of the shell element was made possi-
ble by the continuous development of plate elements. The ﬁrst plate 
element was the Kirchoﬀ plate introduced by Adini and Clough [1]. 
In  the  following  period,  scholars  continued  reﬁning  Kirchhoﬀ  plate 
elements until 1969, when the Discrete Kirchhoﬀ plate and shell ele-
ments appeared [2]. Unfortunately, Kirchoﬀ plates fail to accommodate 
transverse shear in the bending deformation. Thus, Raymond Mindlin’s 
modiﬁcation of the Kirchhoﬀ plate so that it is middle normal remains 
straight after bending and deforming but is not necessarily perpendic-
ular to the middle surface is called Mindlin plate element. Hughes and 
Tezduyar [3] ﬁrst proposed a bilinear four-node quadrilateral shell el-
ement based on Mindlin’s theory in 1977. Ahmad [4] applies the ﬁnite 
element formulation to propose a three-dimensional degenerate curved 
shell  element;  when  the  curvature  of  the  shell  element  is  zero,  the 

formula  is  the  same  as  the  Mindlin  shell  element  formula.  Scholars 
have developed some high-precision high-order quadrilateral degener-
ate shell elements on this basis [5–7]. Although higher-order elements 
usually perform better than lower-order elements in most situations, 
the  calculation  scale  is  much  larger  for  the  same  number  of  meshes 
due to the higher number of nodes. It is a consistent goal of many re-
searchers to build an eﬃcient low-order four-node quadrilateral shell 
element without adding any nodes or degrees of freedom.

The main problems of low-order shell elements exist in various lock-
ing  phenomenon,  including  shear  locking,  membrane  locking,  trans-
verse shear locking, and volume locking. The shear locking is caused by 
the introduction of isoparametric elements, which can not accurately 
describe the in-plane bending deformation 𝑊 = 𝑥2 due to the linear in-
terpolation of the low-order elements [8]. Membrane locking is caused 
by failure to satisfy the inextensibility constraint when the shell ele-
ment bending deformation [9]. Transverse shear locking refers to the 
pseudo-shear phenomenon in the shell element during bending, mak-
ing the deﬂection and bending strain much smaller than the expected 
results. Volume locking is spurious pressure stress occurring when the

Poisson’s ratio is close to 0.5. Many methods have been introduced to 
avoid various locking. For example, the reduced/selective integration 
method [10,11] introduced between 1977 and 1978, the assumed nat-
ural strain method [12] introduced between 1981 and 1982; and the 
subsequent discrete shear gap method [13,14] and the enhanced strain 
method [15,16], etc. Many scholars have developed elements that have 
eliminated various locking phenomena, Katili et al. [17] developed a 
24 dof quadrilateral shell element called DKMQ24 based on the DKMQ 
plate element without shear locking and membrane locking phenom-
ena. Selective reduction of the integral of the shear term was employed 
in  the  DKMQ24  called  DKMQ24+  was  introduced  by  Štembera  and 
Füssl [18].  The  mixed  interpolation  of  tensorial  components  (MITC) 
method [19–21] is  similar  to  a  particular  assumed  strain  method.  It 
eﬀectively eliminates membrane locking and shear locking in apply-
ing  shell  elements,  especially  in  high-order  shell  elements [22–25]. 
However, membrane locking still exists when the 4-node quadrilateral 
MITC4 shell element is calculated for some cured models. Although re-
searchers have made many attempts to modify the strain components 
of the membrane [26,27], these attempts have not shown better results 
and have even demonstrated worse results. Then, the MITC4+ method 
is introduced by Yeongbin et al. [28], which constructs a new assumed 
strain by four non-overlapping triangular strain ﬁelds in the mid-surface 
of the quadrilateral shell element. This method provides an excellent 
solution  to  the  membrane-locking  problem  of  MITC4  elements,  and 
the convergence of MITC4+ elements was veriﬁed by Ko et al. [29],
through a set of widely-used benchmark problems. It shows well the 
performance of MITC4+ elements even under distorted meshes. How-
ever, the MITC4+ element computational scale and the computational 
eﬃciency are still not up to the ideal conditions.

In  the  past  few  decades,  researchers  have  been  keen  to  develop 
reduced integration methods to improve the computational eﬃciency 
of ﬁnite elements. Currently, the most dominant reduced integration 
method for shell element is the one-point integration (at the centre of 
a mid-surface). It is widely used in commercial software for the simula-
tion of collisions and ramming, such as LS-DYNA and RADIOSS, because 
of its superior computational eﬃciency for static and explicit dynamics 
problems. However, zero energy and hourglass models will be induced 
in the centre point of the integral sometimes. For restraining the hour-
glass model, various hourglass control methods were published. Kosloﬀ 
and Frazier [30] ﬁrst attempted hourglass control in a two-dimensional 
quadrilateral,  this  method  was  applied  to  the  shell  element  by  Tay-
lor [31], Hughes and Liu [32]. The introduction of the BT shell [33] was 
a milestone in developing the one-point integral shell element, which 
was popular because of the better stability obtained by adding artiﬁcial 
strain. However, the BT shell had poor results in warping behaviour
and required manual adjustment of the hourglass control factor. Subse-
quently, Belytschko and Leviathan [34] proposed a one-point integral 
four-node  shell  element  based  on  physical  stabilization,  sacriﬁcing  a 
smaller  computational  eﬃciency  to  obtain  better  stability.  Many  re-
searchers [35–37] have  used  this  method  to  derive  a  series  of  shell 
elements.

In this work, a novel one point quadrature quadrilateral shell ele-
ment with the MITC4+ method is developed to improve the compu-

tational eﬃciency of the MITC4+ shell element. The key idea of this 
work is to construct a stabilization part in quadrilateral shell element 
with one point quadrature. The strain in the shell element is divided 
into two components, the membrane strain ﬁeld and the bending strain 
ﬁeld, in the natural coordinate system. A physical stabilization scheme 
is  used  to  decompose  these  two  components  of  the  strain  ﬁeld  into 
the sum of a constant and a sum of linear terms, respectively. In this 
way, the developed MITC4+R element can alleviate the hourglass phe-
nomenon,  which  makes  the  MITC4+R  to  be  more  eﬃcient  than  the 
original MITC4+ without any loss of accuracy. Standard benchmarks 
and practical examples are performed. The results are compared with 
analytical or previously reported results. The performance of the devel-
oped element has great potential for practical applications.

2.2.  The MITC4+ method

The key idea of the MITC4 method is to construct a new assumed 
strain ﬁeld in the mid-surface to eliminate various locking phenomenon 
of the shell element. The new assumed strain ﬁeld is interpolated by 
sampling points (A, B, C, D) located at the centre of each edge of the 
quadrilateral shell with natural coordinates (0, 1, 0), (0, −1, 0), (1, 0, 
0) and (−1, 0, 0), respectively.

non-overlapping 3-node triangular domains divided by point O and four 
nodes of quadrilateral shell as shown in Fig. 2. Point O is deﬁned as the 
centroid of the mid-surface in the shell element, which is calculated 
from the four nodes of the quadrilateral shell, and the detailed deriva-
tion is shown in [28]. The strain in each triangular domain is given

4.3.  Comparison of calculation time

Finally, the computational eﬃciency of MITC4+ and MITC4+R is 
compared. For a better view of the results, the above ﬁve examples of 
explicit dynamics are examined, the calculation time of MITC4+ and 
MITC4+R was counted for each example. The ﬁnal results are shown 
in Table 5. All computation times in the table are in seconds, and the 
letters in the second row of the table indicate the diﬀerent tests: (a) 
shallow spherical dome, (b) twist beam, (c) cylindrical panel, (d) impact 
between two tubes, and (e) ball impact clamped beam, respectively. It 
can be clearly observed that MITC4+R has a higher computational eﬃ-
ciency in the previous four examples. Due to the division of the sphere 
ball into a large number of solid elements, only a weak advantage is 
shown in the last example. In conclusion, the MITC4+R has a higher 
computational rate than the MITC4+ at the same computational scale.
